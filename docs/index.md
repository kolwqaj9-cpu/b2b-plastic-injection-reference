## 5. Mold Making as the Center of the Trade

Preserved shop record for b2b-plastic-injection.com. The former plant is not quoting from this domain. Across the four sections above, one thing keeps coming back: every job at this shop began at the mold. Insert molding, gear molding, prototype insert molding, and multi-cavity work are not separate trades here. They are four ways of asking the same mold maker's question: where does the plastic enter, how does it fill, how does it cool, and how does the part leave the tool without damage. The preserved pages describe a company that majored in plastic injection molding, double plastic injection mold, die casting mold, and gas assistant plastic mould research, so mold making is not a supporting service in this record. It is the core trade, and the molded parts are what the core trade produces. That framing explains the vocabulary of the product pages. The auto part inventory names consul bodies, covers, fillers, filters, and back mirror sets, and every one of those names is a tooling problem before it is a product. A consul cover is a cavity shape, a surface finish requirement, a gate location, and an ejection plan. A filter cover is a thin wall that must fill completely without short shots. A back mirror consul set is an assembly of parts that must come out of separate cavities or separate molds and still fit together. When the preserved text calls the shop a professional mold maker and mold master, it is claiming exactly this: the shop's competence lives in steel, not in plastic. The double injection mold and gas assistant mould research lines point the same way. Both are tooling technologies first, and only afterward production methods. Reading the record this way keeps the index honest. It is a mold maker's archive, and the molded auto, electronic, computer, and motorcycle parts are the evidence of the molds, not the other way around.
